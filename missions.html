How a space mission is designed

A mission begins with a scientific, exploration or operational objective. Engineers then select a spacecraft design, launch vehicle, trajectory, communications system and instruments that can meet the objective within technical and budget constraints.

Why mission phases matter

Launch is only one part of a mission. Depending on its destination, a spacecraft may go through deployment, commissioning, cruise, orbital insertion, surface operations, sample collection or extended science operations. Each phase has different risks and success criteria.

Reading mission information critically

Mission dates and status can change. When a page discusses a future launch, milestone or operational status, check the latest information from the mission operator or space agency. SpaceFleet provides context and links rather than presenting a static page as a live mission feed.

Major mission goals

  • Earth science: understand weather, climate, oceans, land and atmosphere.
  • Planetary science: investigate planets, moons, asteroids and comets.
  • Human exploration: support crewed research and exploration beyond Earth orbit.
  • Space astronomy: observe the universe from locations above Earth's atmosphere.
