How to evaluate a space story

Space news often begins with a technical event: a launch, observation, discovery, anomaly or mission milestone. A useful first step is to identify the primary organization involved and compare the story with its official release or mission page.

Why publication time matters

Launch schedules, weather forecasts and mission status can change. A story that was accurate yesterday may no longer describe the current situation. Look for the article's publication and update time before treating a schedule or status as current.

SpaceFleet's news philosophy

SpaceFleet does not copy articles or present another publisher's reporting as its own. Instead, this page explains how to navigate the wider space-information ecosystem and sends readers to established sources for the original reporting.

Useful source categories

  • Space agencies and mission operators for official mission information.
  • Scientific institutions and observatories for research announcements.
  • Established space publications for reporting and context.
  • Tracking and data services for current technical measurements.
