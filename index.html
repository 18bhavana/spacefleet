Space, explained simply

Our guides focus on concepts that help visitors understand what they see in the sky and what space systems do.

From orbit to observation

Satellites are placed into different orbits because their missions have different requirements. Earth-observation spacecraft may use low orbits to collect detailed images, communications systems may use higher orbits for coverage, and navigation constellations use carefully designed orbital planes.

The same idea applies to space weather. Solar activity begins at the Sun, but its effects can travel through interplanetary space and interact with Earth's magnetic environment. Understanding the chain of events makes alerts and measurements easier to interpret.

Use live sources for changing information

SpaceFleet separates educational explanations from live data. When a visitor needs a current satellite position, ISS pass, solar measurement or mission update, the site points them toward established providers rather than pretending that a static page is a live tracking system.
